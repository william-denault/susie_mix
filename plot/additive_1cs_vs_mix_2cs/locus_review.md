**Biological review of additive_1cs_vs_mix_2cs — 9 September 2026**

I screened all 78 four-panel PNGs (77 distinct genes) and extracted the numerical results from [the plot summary](C:/Document/Serieux/Travail/Data_analysis_and_papers/susie_mix/plot/additive_1cs_vs_mix_2cs/additive_1cs_vs_mix_2cs_plot_summary.csv). I inspected the strongest and Alzheimer's-related examples at larger resolution and checked the relevant biology against primary research.

**My priority is PON1 and C1GALT1 for blood/vascular/immune biology, KCNMA1 and MCPH1 for neurological disease, and LHCGR as a particularly clear example outside those categories. LRRC15 is another promising conditional-signal example, with tumour-stroma and immune relevance.** MS4A14 and HLA-DMA are worth Alzheimer-focused follow-up, but their extra credible sets are poorly localized in this collection.

These are gene-expression fine-mapping examples. A disease connection of the expressed gene does not show that the plotted variants cause that disease. This review establishes biological priorities; it does not establish overlap or linkage disequilibrium with disease-associated variants, colocalization, replication, or superiority of either method against known causal truth.

**How to read the numbers**

A/R/D mean additive/recessive/dominant. PIPs below are for individual SNP–coding predictors, as recorded in the summary. They are not PIPs aggregated over the three codings of a biological SNP. Credible-set sizes count predictor columns, not necessarily distinct biological SNPs. The two entries follow the CSV's cs1/cs2 order, which can differ from the fitted component order (for example, PON1 and KCNMA1).

**The examples I would examine first**

1. **PON1 — adrenal gland.** The recessive predictor at chr7:95,324,583 has PIP 0.999999 and a singleton CS; the additive predictor at chr7:95,324,637 has PIP 0.889 and a two-predictor CS. The recessive genotype groups contain 41/66/34 donors, so that contrast is not driven by one or two rare homozygotes. The conditional plot shows a marked reduction in the homozygous group. This is one of the clearest statistical examples, with relevant plasma/vascular biology. However, the leads are only **54 bp apart**: inspect their joint genotype counts, haplotypes, and raw/coded LD before claiming two independent causal variants. The additive lead has only five minor-allele homozygotes, and the tissue is adrenal gland.

2. **C1GALT1 — thyroid.** Dominant PIP 0.497 with five predictors in its CS, and additive PIP 0.831 with seven. The dominant pattern has 308/72/10 donors; the additive pattern has 117/172/101. The two conditional patterns are readable and both CSs are compact. The strongest disease connection is to **serum IgA1 glycosylation**, a directly measured human immune phenotype, with relevance to IgA nephropathy. It would be premature to describe the thyroid eQTL as explaining kidney disease.

3. **KCNMA1 — blood.** A recessive lead with PIP 0.045 sits in a 31-predictor CS; the dominant lead has PIP 0.414 and a 13-predictor CS. The recessive plot has 17 homozygotes and a clear expression shift. This merits checking because KCNMA1 has established neurological disease biology. The low recessive lead PIP means uncertainty over the causal predictor remains substantial; the CS could still be informative if it is pure. Blood expression does not demonstrate the same mechanism in neurons.

4. **MCPH1 — nerve.** Dominant/recessive lead PIPs 0.348/0.225 and CS sizes 26/45. The recessive plot has 14 homozygotes and a large conditional shift. This is a useful neurodevelopmental follow-up. Its known Mendelian microcephaly mechanism does not by itself validate these common regulatory variants, and the dominant lead has only five homozygotes.

5. **LHCGR — adipose tissue.** Additive/recessive lead PIPs 0.963/0.908 and CS sizes 1/3; the recessive lead has 36 homozygotes. This is one of the most localized examples in the whole folder. Its biological domain is reproductive endocrinology. I would keep it on the shortlist even if the main manuscript discussion emphasizes blood or brain.

6. **LRRC15 — stomach.** Added after the user highlighted this plot. The recessive lead at chr3:194,726,315 has genotype counts 62/100/27. Its homozygous group has higher conditional expression while the other two group means are similar, after adjustment for the dominant lead at chr3:194,366,591. The dominant contrast is supported by 39 heterozygotes, although only four homozygotes make the precise distinction from an additive effect uncertain. Lead PIPs are 0.257/0.109 and CS sizes 32/27. The lower recessive PIP describes uncertainty about the individual causal predictor; it is not a probability that the entire signal exists. The leads are 359,724 bp apart, but that does not establish independence. This is a convincing candidate additional conditional pattern to validate with joint genotype/LD inspection, CS purity, flexible adjustment for the first SNP's three genotype means, covariate checks and replication. Because LRRC15 marks fibroblast states, bulk stomach cell composition is especially relevant. The plot cannot establish that this eQTL is previously unreported.

**Alzheimer's-specific assessment**

- **MS4A14 is the most obvious regional Alzheimer lead in the collection.** The MS4A cluster has strong Alzheimer and soluble-TREM2 evidence, but primary work particularly implicates MS4A4A and MS4A6A. A later proteogenomic fine-mapping study prioritized coding variants in those genes. [Proteogenomics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10763080/). Here the recessive lead PIP is only 0.0268 and the CS contains 2,155 predictors, in adipose tissue. Treat this as a reason to investigate the region, not a convincing new MS4A14 Alzheimer mechanism.
- **HLA-DMA has the relevant tissue label, but the displayed extra signal is weakly localized.** Its dominant CS contains 11,541 predictors and has lead PIP 0.0257. The first lead has genotype counts 215/42/0: at this SNP, additive and dominant genotype vectors are identical in the observed donors. Splitting posterior mass between coding blocks therefore cannot establish different modes of inheritance for that lead. The HLA region has strong Alzheimer/Parkinson evidence, with published fine-mapping supporting HLA-DRB1*04 subtypes; gene assignment to HLA-DMA needs separate evidence.
- **RAB29 is primarily a Parkinson/PARK16 lead for this review.** The neighboring PM20D1 region also has Alzheimer-related methylation and expression research, making a regional follow-up reasonable. That does not transfer the disease assignment to RAB29, or demonstrate overlap with these lead variants. [PM20D1 primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29736028/).
- KCNMA1, MCPH1, GRIN2A, BORCS7 and SNX19 have neurological or psychiatric relevance, but that should not be relabeled as established Alzheimer's causality.

**What the current plots establish, and what needs checking**

The plots show that changing the predictor coding can change fine-mapping output and reveal candidate conditional expression patterns. They do not establish which method has made a false discovery, because the causal truth is unknown in these observational examples. Both sets of boxplots were constructed using predictors selected from the same data; they are descriptive follow-ups rather than independent validation.

There is a concrete CS-quality issue to audit. In this folder, **36/78 examples have at least one mixed CS larger than 1,000 predictors**, while **23/78 have both CSs at most 100 predictors**. Size alone is not a validity test. The current [scan workhorse](C:/Document/Serieux/Travail/Data_analysis_and_papers/susie_mix/script/scan_tissue_attempt/workhorse.R:52) defaults to `min_abs_corr = 0.0`, which imposes no positive within-CS correlation requirement. I have not verified the saved fits' actual parameter settings or purity values. Before counting all extra CSs as discoveries, verify those settings and examine CS purity under a meaningful threshold (for example 0.5), alongside convergence and sensitivity to model settings.

The mixed PIP x-axis concatenates coding blocks. Two widely separated peaks in this display can correspond to nearby or identical biological SNPs. Plot against genomic position for a manuscript figure and report both coding-specific and biological-SNP uncertainty. Four examples (CCDC110, GRIN3B, NPIPA1 and PLA2G4C) have two additive lead codings in the CSV; the folder title alone does not establish a nonadditive lead.

For the shortlist, first audit genotype calls and joint genotype counts, evaluate CS purity and stability, and check the same patterns in independent donors or data. Then compare the full eQTL credible sets with the relevant disease GWAS and test shared-signal evidence. Audit ancestry, tissue subtype and cell-composition adjustment, especially for broadly labeled Brain and Blood Vessel samples. These are selected examples, so their frequency within this folder cannot estimate the prevalence of nonadditive regulation.
